Teacher and mathematician

Two parts of the same professional identity

I do not see being a mathematician and being a teacher as competing identities. Advanced study gives me a wider view of where mathematical ideas come from, how they connect and where they lead.

Classroom teaching requires something complementary: making those ideas accessible, carefully sequenced and meaningful for pupils. Deeper subject knowledge matters most when it improves the quality of what pupils experience.

Good Mathematics teaching should help pupils see both how something works and, when appropriate, why it works.

What matters to me

Professional values

01

Clarity

Precise explanation, notation and expectations make Mathematics more accessible.

02

Intellectual honesty

Explanations should preserve the genuine mathematical idea rather than reduce understanding to rules that merely appear to work.

03

High expectations

Appropriate support should help pupils access worthwhile Mathematics rather than lower the destination.

04

Reflection

Teaching develops through evidence from pupils, professional feedback and a willingness to refine practice.

Beyond the classroom

Mathematics remains part of my wider work

Alongside teaching, I retain active interests in applied mathematics, mathematical physics and research. My previous work includes postgraduate research in electrowetting, numerical and analytical Mathematics, and university-level Mathematics education.

These interests form part of the same broader professional life: teaching Mathematics, understanding Mathematics and continuing to learn Mathematics.
